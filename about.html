SafeJourney is a proposed independent, non-partisan passenger-rights and public-interest network. Our goal is to protect travelers through education, responsible evidence review, fair complaint pathways, community support and lawful institutional reform.

01 / VISION

A world where every traveler is heard, respected and protected.

Regardless of nationality, income, disability, gender, route or mode of transport, every person should have access to clear information, fair treatment and effective complaint mechanisms.

02 / MISSION

Turn passenger experiences into evidence, education and lasting change.

Document concerns safely, assess claims responsibly, explain applicable rights, connect people with appropriate remedies, and advocate for transparent standards across airlines, airports, buses, trains, ferries and travel services.

Our non-negotiable principles

Evidence, not accusation

Allegations are never presented as proven facts without adequate verification and fair opportunity to respond.

People before publicity

Consent, privacy, survivor dignity, safeguarding and data minimization guide every case.

Independent & non-partisan

No party, commercial operator or donor may dictate investigations, endorsements or conclusions.

Lawful remedies

Prioritize official complaints, mediation, consumer protection, regulator engagement and qualified legal referrals.

Accessible to all

Local languages, disability access, affordable participation and equitable regional representation.

Transparency & correction

Publish governance reports, conflicts of interest, financial summaries and clear corrections when needed.

DRAFT CONSTITUTION · VERSION 0.1

How SafeJourney is governed

A practical constitutional outline to be debated, localized and formally adopted by founding members with legal advice in each relevant jurisdiction.

Article 1 — Name, identity and legal status

SafeJourney is the proposed name of a non-profit, public-interest, non-partisan passenger-rights network. A founding assembly will determine its registered legal entity, domicile, jurisdiction and any required country affiliates. No chapter may claim registration before approval.

Article 2 — Objectives and permitted activities

Passenger-rights education; responsible case documentation; referral to regulators and qualified professionals; public research; policy dialogue; accessible awareness campaigns; volunteer training; and lawful cooperation with civic organizations. SafeJourney will not exercise police, judicial or regulatory powers.

Article 3 — Membership and voting

Proposed categories: individual, volunteer, student, professional, institutional supporter and honorary. Voting eligibility should require verified membership, good standing and a minimum participation period defined by bylaws. Each eligible individual has one vote; institutional supporters and donors have no automatic voting power.

Article 4 — General Assembly

Meet at least annually, including secure remote participation. Approve audited accounts, elect directors, review impact and vote on reserved matters. Provide at least 30 days' notice; a proposed quorum is 25% of eligible voters or accredited delegates, subject to local law and final adoption.

Article 5 — Board elections and accountability

Directors are elected through an independently supervised ballot for staggered three-year terms, with no more than two consecutive terms. Vacancies are filled temporarily until the next assembly. Removal requires due process and a supermajority board or assembly vote under adopted bylaws.

Article 6 — Officers, committees and executive

The board appoints its officers and hires an Executive Director through a transparent process. Finance, Audit & Risk, Ethics & Safeguarding, and Nominations committees must have written mandates. The Chair and Executive Director should not be the same person.

Article 7 — Chapters and territorial recognition

Country councils and local chapters operate under a charter, training, code of conduct, reporting rules and periodic accreditation. They may not issue binding legal advice, collect unrestricted funds, use the name for partisan campaigns or publish unreviewed allegations.

Article 8 — Complaints, investigations and appeals

Separate intake, triage, evidence verification, right of reply, editorial approval, correction and appeal. Sensitive cases use secure access controls, retention schedules and informed consent. Serious threats go to appropriate emergency or regulatory authorities.

Article 9 — Finance and anti-corruption

Permitted funding may include small donations, grants, memberships and ethical training revenue. Require budgets, dual payment authorization, conflict declarations, restricted-fund tracking, procurement rules and published annual financial reports. No donor may purchase favorable case outcomes.

Article 10 — Safeguarding, privacy and conduct

Prohibit harassment, retaliation, discrimination, bribery, fabrication and misuse of personal data. Maintain confidential reporting, whistleblower protections, independent disciplinary review and proportionate sanctions with appeal rights.

Article 11 — Amendments and continuity

Constitutional amendments require advance publication, member consultation and at least a two-thirds vote of the General Assembly, subject to applicable law. Mission, non-profit character, non-partisanship and independence require stronger safeguards in final bylaws.

Article 12 — Succession, emergency powers and dissolution

Maintain leadership succession plans, archived records, institutional handover and an emergency board quorum. Emergency powers expire automatically unless ratified. Upon dissolution, remaining assets go to a legally eligible organization with similar public-benefit purposes, never to founders or directors personally.

These clauses are illustrative proposals, not adopted legal articles. Quorum, voting rights, director duties, privacy obligations and charity status must be reviewed against the law of the registration country.

THE 100-YEAR INSTITUTIONAL PLAN

Designed for 2126 — not just today.

A century-long direction, with periodic independent reviews so future members can adapt the methods while protecting the mission.

2026–2030

Foundation

Convene founding members, adopt the constitution, register legally, establish safeguards, pilot case workflows and launch initial local chapters.

2031–2040

National networks

Train local leaders, establish country councils where legally viable, build multilingual rights resources and publish annual impact reviews.

2041–2050

International collaboration

Expand cross-border referrals, research partnerships, accessibility standards and regional passenger protection forums.

2051–2075

Institutional resilience

Strengthen reserves, archives, succession, independent audit, youth leadership and technology renewal across generations.

2076–2100

Public knowledge legacy

Maintain a responsibly governed passenger-rights knowledge archive, comparative policy research and community training institutions.

2101–2126

Centennial stewardship

Renew the mandate through member deliberation, safeguard independence and transfer leadership to future generations.

Every year

Publish activity, financial and safeguarding reports; hold an assembly; review chapters and leadership performance.

Every 5 years

Commission an external impact and governance review; refresh the strategic plan and risk controls.

Every 25 years

Hold a constitutional convention with member and chapter representation to review long-term relevance.

GLOBAL CHAPTER NETWORK

Start where passengers need support.

A proposed local chapter model for countries, cities, university campuses and transport communities. Chapters must be approved and trained before using the SafeJourney name officially.

Country Council

Coordinates legally compliant national operations, local-language materials, training, regulator relations and chapter standards.

City / District Chapter

Runs passenger-awareness sessions, information desks, community listening meetings and verified referral directories.

Campus / Volunteer Chapter

Builds youth leadership, multilingual outreach, research assistance and responsible digital campaigns.

Travel safety awareness

Run multilingual workshops at campuses and communities on documentation, scams, safety and respectful travel.

Evidence & policy research

Aggregate anonymized trends with consent, publish careful findings and propose service improvements.
